# Common jobs

Read only the entry for the job the owner is describing, to know what to probe. Never walk the owner through this list.

"Live" work happens in real time, like a phone call. A mistake reaches the customer before anyone can step in. "Drafted" work waits for a person to check it before it goes out. Drafted work is the safer first pilot.

## 1. Missed calls and after-hours inquiries

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** usually nobody is free to answer, not a lack of software. Even if every message is captured, someone still has to call back. Check who does, and when. Hang-ups who leave no message are often the biggest loss.
- **No-AI fix:** a clear callback promise on the greeting, an answering service or overflow line, one person owning the callback list, or texting back missed numbers automatically from the phone system.
- **AI fit:** strong for drafting replies to texts, web forms and voicemails, and for sorting urgent from routine. Live AI phone answering is a bigger step: it needs a real-time handoff to a person. Red lines are safety calls (gas smell, no heat with vulnerable people, symptoms) and quoting prices.
- **Usual value:** revenue won or kept; time back for whoever works the callback list.
- **Follow-ups:** "When someone hangs up without leaving a message, what happens?" "On a busy day, how long until a missed caller hears back?" "Who calls them back while you're on a job?"

## 2. Quotes, proposals and lead follow-up

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** quotes wait for the owner's time; nobody follows up once a quote is sent; past jobs aren't recorded well enough to price from.
- **No-AI fix:** a follow-up reminder on every sent quote in the job or CRM tool, quote templates for common jobs, and a set day for quoting.
- **AI fit:** drafting quotes or proposals from notes and photos, and drafting follow-ups on quiet quotes. A person always checks prices and scope before anything is sent. Red lines are committing to a price or deadline on its own.
- **Usual value:** revenue won or kept (quotes that would have gone cold); time back for the owner.
- **Follow-ups:** "Of the last ten quotes, how many did you hear nothing back on?" "Does anyone follow up, and after how long?" "Could you price a job from what's in your system, or is it in your head?"

## 3. Booking, rescheduling and no-shows

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** back-and-forth to find a time, reschedules handled by phone, no reminders, or a cancellation policy nobody enforces.
- **No-AI fix:** online booking, automatic reminders and confirmations in the booking tool, and a deposit or cancellation policy. These are usually Not AI.
- **AI fit:** handling reschedule requests that arrive by text or email in plain language, and filling cancellations from a waitlist. A person handles exceptions to policy and fee waivers unless the owner sets a limit.
- **Usual value:** revenue won or kept (fewer empty slots); time back at the front desk.
- **Follow-ups:** "How many no-shows or late cancellations in a typical week?" "Do reminders go out now, and how?" "What happens to a slot when someone cancels the same day?"

## 4. Client or patient intake and onboarding

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** forms filled in on arrival or by phone, information re-typed into systems, and missing details chased later.
- **No-AI fix:** online forms sent automatically at booking, required fields, and a checklist for each new client.
- **AI fit:** summarizing what a client sent before the first visit or meeting, spotting missing information, and drafting welcome and prep messages. Red lines are clinical judgment or advice, sharing health or financial details beyond who needs them, and anything that sounds like a diagnosis.
- **Usual value:** time back; noted benefits such as smoother first visits and fewer surprises.
- **Follow-ups:** "What do you usually find out too late about a new client?" "Who re-types what, and into where?" "What's sensitive in what they send you?"

## 5. Customer inbox, returns and refunds

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** the same handful of questions every day ("where's my order?", hours, policies), returns needing a decision, and replies waiting until someone has a free moment.
- **No-AI fix:** an order-status page or automatic shipping emails, a clear returns page, saved replies, and a returns portal in the store platform.
- **AI fit:** strong for drafting answers to repeat questions from the business's own policies, and sorting what needs a person. Refunds, credits and exceptions go to a person, or run on their own only up to a limit the owner sets. Red lines are promises outside policy and anything that moves money above the limit.
- **Usual value:** time back; money not lost (wrong refunds, chargebacks from slow replies); noted benefits such as faster replies and better reviews.
- **Follow-ups:** "What are the three questions you answer most?" "How long does a customer wait for a reply on a busy day?" "In the last year, how much went out in refunds or chargebacks that a quicker or clearer reply would have avoided?"

## 6. Invoice chasing and collections

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** invoices sent late, no reminders, and nobody comfortable chasing. Some debts end up written off.
- **No-AI fix:** invoice on completion, automatic reminders in the invoicing tool, card on file or deposits, and clear payment terms. Often mostly Not AI.
- **AI fit:** drafting personal follow-ups for overdue accounts the automatic reminders didn't move, summarizing who owes what and why, and preparing calls. Payment plans, discounts and fee waivers go to a person, or run on their own up to a limit the owner sets.
- **Usual value:** money not lost (write-offs avoided); time back. Getting paid sooner helps cash flow, but count only write-offs in dollars and note the speed.
- **Follow-ups:** "How much did you write off last year?" "Do reminders go out automatically now?" "Who chases, and how do they feel about it?"

## 7. Client updates, reporting and field summaries

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** notes scattered across texts, photos and heads; updates written from scratch; reports built by hand each week or month.
- **No-AI fix:** one place for job notes and photos, a fixed update template, and a scheduled report from the tool's own reporting.
- **AI fit:** strong for turning rough notes, photos or data into a clear client update, job summary or report draft that a person checks before sending. Red lines are promises about dates or results that a person hasn't confirmed.
- **Usual value:** time back (often the owner's evenings); noted benefits such as happier clients and fewer "what's happening?" calls.
- **Follow-ups:** "When did you last write one of these, and how long did it take?" "Where do the notes come from?" "What do clients complain they don't hear about?"

## 8. Back-office admin, reviews and reactivation

- **Root cause and bottleneck:** data entered twice, end-of-day tidying, review requests forgotten, and past customers never contacted again.
- **No-AI fix:** connect the tools so data moves once; turn on the platform's automatic review requests and reactivation or service-reminder campaigns. Reviews and reactivation are usually Not AI.
- **AI fit:** tidying messy notes into the right fields, drafting replies to reviews (checked before posting), and personal reactivation messages where a template would feel cold.
- **Usual value:** time back; revenue won or kept from returning customers.
- **Follow-ups:** "What do you type in more than once?" "Do you ask for reviews now, and how?" "How do past customers hear from you?"
