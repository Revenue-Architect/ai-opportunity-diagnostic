# Review of the v0.4 spec (Gemini 4 Neon High, 2026-11-22)

All 12 findings were accepted and folded into docs/v0.4-spec.md.

The three named interview methods in [v0.4-spec.md](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md) (JTBD "last time" story, SPIN implication questions, and end-to-end case tracing) and the four-bucket value model (time, revenue, losses avoided, noted-not-priced) are the right backbone. The issues below are where the draft would still misfire in a real owner conversation, inflate or crash the script math, or fail its own pass criteria.

---

### Findings

#### 1. `MUST` — Section 1 (`Conversation design`): `SKILL.md` stages still enforce the old interrogation pipeline, and the new flow still exceeds 20 questions

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 1](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L26-L55) (and [SKILL.md — Stages](../../SKILL.md#L20-L41))
- **Problem:** Section 1 says changes go in `SKILL.md` *(rules)* and `references/interview.md`, leaving `SKILL.md`'s **Stages** untouched. Those stages still force Intake → No-AI check → Score → Evidence across all jobs, and Stage 4 still tells the agent to ask for CSV exports on the top 3 scored rows (even when a job already failed `data_ready` or the owner is in a van and doesn't want it). Worse, adding story and implication questions without cutting redundant gate prompts still produces ~22–28 questions (2 context + 5 story/follow-ups + 5 sizing + 5 setup + 1 CSV + 3 `ai_tool`/`never`/`tone` prompts, the last two currently hidden in [report.md](../../references/report.md#L39)).
- **Exact change:** Update Section 1 to state that `SKILL.md`'s **Stages** are rewritten alongside its rules:
  1. Merge Intake + No-AI check into one conversational discovery stage; run **Evidence** (30-day CSV) *only* for a candidate job where real volume would actually change the decision (cut the blanket "top 3 scored rows" request).
  2. Add three compression rules to `interview.md`: never re-ask what the owner already answered; skip money-limit questions when money isn't in scope; and confirm obvious setup defaults for draft-only jobs in **one** conversational check (approver, reversibility, rollback, and anything it must never touch).
  3. Move the `never` and `tone` questions out of `references/report.md` into `references/interview.md` (only asked when writing an assistant brief), keeping one home per fact.

#### 2. `MUST` — Section 1 (`Conversation design`): Step 4 misses early advice when the pain isn't a software toggle, ignores "handle everything" hype, and leaves no bridge to a second job

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 1, Steps 2–5](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L37-L46)
- **Problem:** A seasoned small-business ops consultant gives useful perspective by question 5–6 even when there is no "Not AI" toggle to point to. Right now Step 4 only offers early value *"if a setting or rule fixes something (Not AI)."* When Dana opens with missed calls or Priya opens with *"I want an AI receptionist that handles everything,"* neither is a quick setting fix—so the flow has no instruction to deliver an early diagnostic reframe or to test "handle everything" hype against a real case before narrowing scope. Also, Steps 2–3 trace *one* story and Step 5 jumps to *"Pick the focus: 1–2 jobs (3 at most)"* with no step to uncover a second pain (where both Dana's Jobber reminders and Priya's Jane reminders came from).
- **Exact change:** Revise Steps 2–5 in Section 1:
  - In Step 2, add: **If the owner leads with a pre-baked AI pitch ("handle everything", "answer the phone"), test it on their real story** (*"Let's trace what that assistant would actually have done on your last hectic lunchtime"*) instead of ignoring it or silently pivoting to drafts.
  - Replace Step 4 with: **"Give an early read by question 5–6."** Name the real bottleneck you're hearing, separate what AI can safely touch from what needs a person or process fix, and call out any Not-AI setting or rule fix as soon as it appears.
  - Before Step 5, add **one brief sweep question**: *"Besides [pain 1], is there one other thing eating the week—like reminders, quotes, or chasing bills—or is [pain 1] the whole reason we're talking?"*

#### 3. `MUST` — Section 3 (`Value model`): Time-only requirements crash revenue/loss jobs, draft time savings ignore review overhead, and the double-counting rule is backwards

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 3](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L80-L92) (and [diagnose.py](../../scripts/diagnose.py#L74-L86))
- **Problem:** Three logic errors in Section 3:
  1. Section 1 step 7 says *"Ask only about the value drivers the story points to,"* yet `diagnose.py` requires `loaded_hourly_cost`, `weekly_volume`, `minutes_per_item`, and `share_handled` on every non-`Not AI` job. A revenue-only job (e.g., callers who hang up with zero current prep time) or a losses-only job will raise `InputError`.
  2. For drafted work (like Priya's inbox replies), multiplying full `minutes_per_item × share_handled` counts 100% of the minutes as saved even though staff still read, edit, and send every draft—explicitly called out in [the v0.2 roleplay critique](../../docs/reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md#L566) and still unfixed.
  3. The stated double-counting rule (*"minutes saved on an item that's also counted as recovered revenue are not counted twice"*) is wrong: saving labour cost and winning incremental margin on the same lead queue are distinct effects (and `assets/example-case.json` counts both). The *real* double-counting traps are: (a) pricing owner time at extra billable profit while also counting recovered revenue that uses those same hours, (b) counting rework in `minutes_per_item` and again in `losses_avoided_annual`, and (c) counting a refunded/botched order in both `recovered_revenue_annual` and `losses_avoided_annual`. Repeating the rule in both `interview.md` and `common-jobs.md` also breaks one home per fact.
- **Exact change:** In Section 3:
  - Make time fields (`weekly_volume`, `minutes_per_item`, `share_handled`) and `loaded_hourly_cost` optional when time is not a priced driver (require at least one priced driver—or an explicit `unpriced_drivers` entry—on every non-`Not AI` job).
  - Define `share_handled` for drafted work as **net share of time actually removed after review and sending** (in `interview.md`: *"Once someone checks and sends the draft, roughly how much of those 8 minutes actually goes away?"*).
  - Replace the double-counting sentence with the three real traps above, and give the rule **one** home (`references/interview.md`).

#### 4. `MUST` — Section 3 (`Value model`): Pricing owner time at "what an hour of that work earns" invites 3×–5× ROI inflation

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 3, Item 1](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L84)
- **Problem:** Asking *"what would you do with those hours?"* and using *"what an hour of that work earns"* will pull in a **gross billing rate** (e.g., $140/hr plumbing rate or $175/hr agency rate) instead of **net margin per billable hour**, contradicting [plan.md](../../docs/plan.md#L15-L18)'s core "value at margin" principle. It also ignores **time fragmentation**: saving 3 minutes across 18 scattered voicemails cannot turn into a billable job unless the business has real unmet demand *and* the saved time comes back in usable blocks. Without guardrails, owner-done jobs will inflate dramatically and turn weak pilots into false `Go` verdicts.
- **Exact change:** Replace the owner-time sentence in Section 3 item 1 with: *"Use an owner rate above the staff replacement rate only when all three hold: (1) they have real unmet billable demand, (2) the saved time comes back in usable blocks (not scattered minutes), and (3) the rate is **net profit/margin per billable hour** (after materials, sub-costs, and non-billable travel), not the gross billing rate—and does not overlap with `recovered_revenue_annual`. Otherwise use the staff replacement rate (or list 'evenings back' under `noted_benefits`). Record the basis in `hourly_value_basis`."* Add one note that avoided hiring is valued at the staff replacement rate, not added separately.

#### 5. `MUST` — Section 3 (`Value model`): The `evidence: "export"` fix still lets a volume CSV stamp `1.00` certainty onto unverified estimates and misstates how to resolve hours conflicts

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 3, Evidence fix](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L93)
- **Problem:** Always running the `hours_claims` conflict check fixes half the bug, but leaves two holes exposed by Priya's roleplay:
  1. Priya and her desk disagreed (4.5 h vs. 8 h) on **scope** (typing replies vs. manning the inbox amid interruptions), not on count ([roleplay line 383](../../docs/reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md#L383)). Saying *"until owner and staff agree after seeing the count (the agent then updates `hours_claims`)"*—while [diagnose.py line 117](../../scripts/diagnose.py#L117) still says `"settle it with real records"`—misleads the agent into thinking a count CSV resolves a time/scope disagreement or nudging the owner to edit `hours_claims` unilaterally.
  2. When there is no conflict (including any solo owner with no staff!), `profile_csv.py` only proves `weekly_volume`, yet `evidence: "export"` still applies weight `1.00` to *every* driver—including unverified `recovered_revenue_annual`, `losses_avoided_annual`, `minutes_per_item`, and `share_handled`. That is why Priya's projected value jumped *up* from `$3,123` to `$3,844` even as verified volume dropped from 35 to 28.
- **Exact change:** Update the Evidence fix in Section 3:
  - Change the `hours:` blocker text in `diagnose.py` to: `"reconcile what both sides are counting and confirm with a quick time check"` (not `"settle it with real records"`), and state that `hours_claims` is updated only after both sides agree on the same scope.
  - Apply the `export` weight (`1.00`) only to `labour` when volume came from an export and hours do not conflict (and in prose/output, still label minutes and share as estimates). Keep unverified `revenue` and `losses` weighted at `single_estimate` (`0.65`) unless they are also backed by records (no new weight constants needed, preserving [plan.md](../../docs/plan.md#L47) and existing trap intent).

#### 6. `MUST` — Section 3 (`Value model`) & Section 1 (Step 8): Missing input contract for `"not priced"` drivers and unpursued focus jobs

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 1, Step 8](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L49) and [Section 3, Output](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L89)
- **Problem:** Two underspecified handoffs between the interview and `diagnose.py`:
  1. `diagnose.py` cannot tell whether an omitted revenue/loss field means **"not applicable"** (reminders don't lose sales) or **"relevant, couldn't estimate"** (Dana's missed calls). Without an explicit field, the script either prints noisy `"not priced"` lines on every job or leaves `"not priced"` to prose (untestable in `test_diagnose.py`).
  2. Step 8 collects `approver`, `rollback`, `data_ready`, and `on_own_is_reversible` *only for the job the owner wants to pursue*. If a second focus job is also put into the JSON without those fields, `diagnose.py` will stamp false blockers (`"no named approver; no same-day rollback; records not usable yet"`) onto it.
- **Exact change:**
  - Add an optional per-job `unpriced_drivers` dict in Section 3 (e.g., `{"revenue": "owner couldn't estimate"}`) so `diagnose.py` prints `"not priced (...)"` only for drivers that actually apply.
  - Clarify in Section 1 Step 8: pass only pursued jobs and `Not AI` quick wins to `diagnose.py`; mention any deprioritized pains in one line in the report so the script never generates artificial missing-gate blockers.

#### 7. `MUST` — Pass criteria & Section 5 (`Tests and checks`): Criterion #4 pressures the agent to oversell, and a single-session self-played roleplay is biased and overfitted

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Pass criteria](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L17-L24) and [Section 5](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L105-L114)
- **Problem:**
  1. Criterion #4 (*"Both simulated owners would book a follow-up"*) fights the Skill's core integrity rule (`"Not yet" is a valid answer`). If Dana's best move is turning on Jobber reminders and testing a human answering service, she shouldn't have to book an AI follow-up for v0.4 to pass.
  2. Criterion #1 doesn't define "useful advice" (generic empathy shouldn't count), and no criterion checks whether staff fear/confidentiality was handled or whether owner-rate/revenue numbers stayed grounded.
  3. Playing both sides in one model session leaks the v0.4 spec and pass criteria to the simulated owner (making them artificially cooperative), and testing *only* Dana and Priya overfits to the two cases that shaped the spec while leaving `losses_avoided_annual`, owner `hourly_value`, stores, and agencies untested.
- **Exact change:**
  - Replace Pass Criterion #4 with: **"Both owners feel understood (no frustration cues like 'is this a time study?') and leave with a concrete next step they'd actually take—booking a follow-up when agenticworks.ca fits, or taking a clear non-AI action while trusting agenticworks.ca enough to return later."**
  - Clarify Criterion #1 as *"a concrete Not-AI fix or bottleneck reframe specific to their business"*, and add to Criterion #5: *"Staff concerns, when raised, are addressed before continuing and kept anonymous in the report."*
  - In Section 5, require **two isolated sessions** (zero new infrastructure: Session A loads only the Skill; Session B gets only a hidden owner brief with pushback/unknowns and never sees the Skill or pass criteria), and add **one unseen holdout persona**—a small store or agency that tests `losses_avoided_annual` (or refund limits) and owner `hourly_value`.

#### 8. `SHOULD` — Section 2 (`references/common-jobs.md`): Rebalance the 8 jobs for stores and agencies, tighten to 5 bullets with missing bottleneck/red-line depth, and prevent checklist drift

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 2](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L57-L78)
- **Problem:** A short reference file is the right lightweight way to add depth, but three things are off:
  1. **Vertical balance:** Small stores (orders, returns, refunds, chargebacks—the exact domain of the repo's own trap tests) and agencies (proposals, client reporting/status updates) are under-served, while #4 ("Intake forms and appointment prep") is clinic-narrow and #7 ("Reviews and reactivating past customers") is mostly a built-in CRM/booking-tool rule (Not AI).
  2. **Missing per-job content:** The 7 bullets omit the three things that mattered most in the roleplays: the **downstream bottleneck check** (who calls back? who handles hang-ups?), **typical red lines / never-touch boundaries** (PHI/symptoms, gas/furnace safety, refunds), and **non-AI operational fixes** beyond software toggles (answering service, staggered lunch coverage). Meanwhile `usual root causes`, `where it goes wrong`, and `live vs. drafted` overlap and crowd the 200-line cap.
  3. **Checklist risk:** Without a guardrail, *"reads it during the conversation to know what to probe"* invites the agent to march through all 8 jobs.
- **Exact change:**
  - Adjust the 8 jobs to cover all four verticals: (1) Missed calls and after-hours inquiries, (2) Quotes, proposals and lead follow-up, (3) Booking, rescheduling and no-shows, (4) Client/patient intake and onboarding, (5) Customer inbox, returns and refunds, (6) Invoice chasing and collections, (7) Client updates, reporting and field summaries, (8) Back-office admin, reviews and reactivation (flagging reviews/reactivation as usually Not AI).
  - Consolidate the 7 bullets into 5 tight fields per job (staying under ~180 lines): *Root cause & bottleneck check*, *Not-AI fix (rule or process)*, *AI fit, live vs. drafted & red lines*, *Usual value drivers*, *2–3 sharp follow-up questions*.
  - Add one explicit rule in `interview.md`: **"Consult only the 1–2 jobs matching what the owner raised; never use `common-jobs.md` as a checklist."**

#### 9. `SHOULD` — Section 1 & Section 4 (`Report`): Define how to close, handle self-serve vs. consultant-led use, and give staff concerns a home in the report

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 1, Step 9](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L50) and [Section 4](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L99-L103)
- **Problem:**
  1. **Closing:** Dana refused a follow-up largely because the report ended with *"Contact agenticworks.ca for a quote once the recordings are accessible"*—a quote for a job she didn't want ([roleplay line 183–185](../../docs/reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md#L183-L185)). Section 4 leaves [report.md Section 6](../../references/report.md#L78-L80) unchanged, and Section 1 has no closing beat.
  2. **Self-serve vs. consultant-led:** A solo owner running the Skill in 20 minutes from a van cannot pull staff aside or export CSVs mid-chat, whereas a consultant running a live engagement can. The spec doesn't tell the agent to adapt when the owner sets a time limit or is running solo.
  3. **Orphaned staff findings:** Step 9 says *"record them as a finding,"* but Section 4 never puts staff concerns in `report.md` or requires staff anonymity (Priya explicitly asked *"Can we leave her name out of it?"*).
- **Exact change:**
  - Add a closing step to Section 1 and update `report.md` Section 6: ask one brief reality-check before/with the report (*"Does this line up with what you're seeing?"*), and match the agenticworks.ca next step to the owner's actual problem (never pitch a quote for a job they didn't want; for "Not yet", offer a short call only if they want help sorting out the root bottleneck or blocker).
  - Add two lines to `interview.md`: respect an owner's stated time cap, and in self-serve mode (no staff present, no CSV handy) note staff confirmation or export verification cleanly under *What would change the answer* instead of stalling the conversation.
  - Add a bullet to Section 4: record staff concerns anonymously under *Case against* or *What would change the answer*, with how to bring staff into the pilot.

#### 10. `SHOULD` — Section 1 & Section 3: Account for seasonality spikes and define `losses_avoided_annual` as net cash loss

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 1, Step 6](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L47) and [Section 3, Item 3](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L86)
- **Problem:** Both roleplay owners raised seasonality—Dana's *"first cold week / if it snows, throw that answer out"* and Priya's *"More in January"*—which the spec ignores (risking 52× peak-week inflation or overlooking peak-season failure). Separately, `losses_avoided_annual` doesn't state whether to use net cash loss or gross retail price, inviting overstatement on refunds.
- **Exact change:**
  - In Section 1 Step 6, add: use a typical week for the 52-week math (never multiply a peak week by 52) and capture seasonal spikes in `noted_benefits`.
  - In Section 3 Item 3, specify: count **actual out-of-pocket cash loss or uncollected cost/margin** (write-offs, chargebacks, scrapped parts, non-recoverable refunds), not gross list price already counted under revenue.

#### 11. `COULD` — Section 3 & `scripts/diagnose.py`: Suppress single-job quadrant charts and fix the x-axis label

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 3](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L89) (and [diagnose.py — quadrant_chart](../../scripts/diagnose.py#L161-L187))
- **Problem:** Flagged in [Priya's critique](../../docs/reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md#L565): plotting a 1-dot quadrant chart adds clutter without comparing anything, and labeling the x-axis `"Harder to build --> Easier to build"` is inaccurate because the script actually plots `1 - build_cost / year_one_value` (build cost relative to value).
- **Exact change:** Emit the Mermaid `quadrantChart` only when 2 or more jobs have quotes, and rename the x-axis to `"Higher build cost vs value --> Lower build cost vs value"`.

#### 12. `COULD` — Section 3 & Section 4: Specify where the per-driver breakdown and `noted_benefits` print in `--markdown`

- **Spec section:** [v0.4-spec.md — Section 3](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L89) and [Section 4](../../docs/v0.4-spec.md#L101)
- **Problem:** The spec requires each driver, its basis, `"not priced"` drivers, and `noted_benefits` in the output, but doesn't say whether they go inside the 7-column summary table (which would break readability) or below it.
- **Exact change:** State in Section 3/4 that `diagnose.py --markdown` keeps the 7-column summary table as-is and prints a short bulleted value breakdown (priced drivers + basis, `not priced` drivers, and `noted_benefits`) underneath the table for each non-`Not AI` job.

---

### Verdict

**Revise the spec before building.** v0.4 diagnoses the v0.2 failures accurately and picks the right lightweight architecture (story-led discovery, a compact common-jobs guide, and a four-part value model with no new outcomes or infrastructure), but building it as written would still break on seven concrete issues: `SKILL.md` stages fighting the new flow and keeping question counts over 20, no early diagnostic reframe when a pain isn't a software toggle, `diagnose.py` crashing on revenue-only jobs and over-crediting draft review time, owner hourly value risking gross-rate inflation, volume exports still stamping `1.00` trust onto unverified guesses, an unspecified schema for `"not priced"` and unpursued jobs, and a follow-up pass criterion + single-session roleplay that reward overselling and overfitting. Tightening the spec with the seven `MUST` fixes (and the three `SHOULD` fixes) will make v0.4 smaller, sharper, and genuinely ready to build and test.
