# v0.4 spec: conversational, deeper, fuller value

Status: built and released, 2026-11-22. Roleplay results: [reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.4.md](reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.4.md). Draft reviewed by Gemini 4 Neon High ([reviews/2026-11-22-gemini-v0.4-spec-review.md](reviews/2026-11-22-gemini-v0.4-spec-review.md)); all 12 findings folded in.

## Why

The roleplay test of v0.2 ([reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md](reviews/2026-11-22-codex-roleplay-v0.2.md)) found:

- **Interrogation.** 23–25 questions before anything useful, 33–36 in total. Cost, margin and recapture were asked before any problem was described.
- **No follow-ups where they mattered.** The agent skipped hang-ups, callback capacity and staff fear.
- **Wrong problem solved.** Both owners came about the phone.
- **Narrow value.** Only time (all at the staff wage) and recovered revenue.
- **Scoring bug.** A volume export stamped full trust onto every estimate and skipped the hours check.

## Pass criteria

Checked by an isolated roleplay (see section 6).

1. First useful advice within about 6 agent questions. That means a concrete Not-AI fix or a reframe of the bottleneck specific to the business, not generic empathy.
2. About 20 agent questions or fewer in total.
3. The recommendation addresses the problem the owner raised, or says plainly why not and what would.
4. Each owner feels understood (no frustration cues like "is this a time study?") and leaves with a concrete next step they'd actually take. That can be a follow-up when agenticworks.ca fits, or a clear non-AI action. "Not yet" can pass.
5. No field names or jargon are spoken to the owner. Staff concerns, when raised, are addressed before continuing and kept anonymous in the report. Totals and outcomes come from the script.
6. All script tests pass.

## 1. Conversation design

This rewrites `SKILL.md` (rules and stages) and `references/interview.md`. The methods behind it:

- the "tell me about the last time" story from jobs-to-be-done switch interviews
- implication questions from SPIN ("what happens then?")
- tracing one real case end to end, from value-stream mapping

**Stages in `SKILL.md`:**

1. **Discover.** Combines the old intake and No-AI check into one conversation.
2. **Focus and size.** Only the chosen jobs.
3. **Set up.** Only the job the owner wants to pursue.
4. **Score.**
5. **Evidence.** Only where real records would change the decision.
6. **Case against.**
7. **Close and report.**

**Flow:**

1. **Two context questions at most:** what the business does, and how many people. Respect any time limit the owner states.
2. **Open on a story:** "What slips through the cracks or eats your week the most?" Then "Tell me about the last time that happened." If the owner leads with an AI pitch ("handle everything", "answer the phone"), test it on their real story: "Let's trace what that assistant would have done last lunchtime."
3. **Follow the pain.** Trace that case end to end: who touched it, where it stalled, why, and what the customer experienced. Ask a follow-up when an answer is:
   - vague
   - surprising
   - emotional
   - a cause

   Ask "what happens then?" to find the cost. Check the downstream bottleneck: who acts on the result?
4. **Give an early read by question 5–6.** Name the real bottleneck you're hearing. Separate what AI can safely touch from what needs a person or a process fix. Call out any Not-AI setting or rule fix as soon as it appears.
5. **One sweep:** "Besides [pain 1], is there one other thing eating the week, or is that the whole reason we're talking?"
6. **Pick the focus with the owner:** 1–2 jobs, 3 at most.
7. **Size roughly, only for the focus jobs,** and only the value drivers the story points to. Use a typical week, never a peak week × 52; note seasonal spikes. Simple arithmetic out loud is fine to check understanding. Assumptions are allowed when stated and confirmed by the owner. Never invent a number.
8. **Set up the pursued job only,** in as few turns as possible. Confirm obvious defaults for draft-only work in one check (approver, easy to undo, switch-back, anything it must never touch). Skip money-limit questions when no money moves. Ask tone and the AI tool here.
9. **If staff concerns come up, address them before continuing.** Record them anonymously.
10. **Close:** one reality check ("Does this line up with what you're seeing?"), then the report.

**Compression rules:**
- Never re-ask what the owner already said.
- Use the common-jobs reference only for the 1–2 jobs the owner raised; never as a checklist.
- In self-serve mode (no staff present, no export to hand), don't stall. Note what staff confirmation or records would change under "What would change the answer".

**Rules that change:**
- "One question per message" becomes "one main question per message; a short reflection before it is fine."
- "Never compute in prose" becomes "simple arithmetic out loud is fine to check understanding; totals, rankings and outcomes come only from the script."
- Field names are never spoken.

**Script input:** pass only the pursued jobs plus Not-AI quick wins to `diagnose.py`. Other pains raised get one line in the report.

## 2. Depth: `references/common-jobs.md` (new, under about 180 lines)

Eight jobs covering trades, clinics, stores and agencies:
1. Missed calls and after-hours inquiries
2. Quotes, proposals and lead follow-up
3. Booking, rescheduling and no-shows
4. Client or patient intake and onboarding
5. Customer inbox, returns and refunds
6. Invoice chasing and collections
7. Client updates, reporting and field summaries
8. Back-office admin, reviews and reactivation (reviews and reactivation are usually Not AI)

Five fields each:
- **Root cause and bottleneck check**
- **Not-AI fix**, either a rule or setting, or a process fix such as an answering service or staggered lunch cover
- **AI fit:** live versus drafted work, and red lines (health details, safety calls, refunds)
- **Usual value drivers**
- **2–3 sharp follow-up questions**

## 3. Value model (`scripts/diagnose.py`)

Annual value = time + revenue + money not lost. Each part is weighted by its own evidence; then first-year build and run cost are subtracted. At least one driver must be priced, or listed in `unpriced_drivers`.

1. **Time back** (optional fields: `weekly_volume`, `minutes_per_item`, `share_handled`; `loaded_hourly_cost` is needed only when time is priced).
   - For drafted work, `share_handled` is the net share of time actually removed after someone checks and sends: "Once someone checks and sends the draft, how much of those 8 minutes actually goes away?"
   - Optional per-job `hourly_value` overrides `loaded_hourly_cost`, with the reason in `hourly_value_basis`. Use an owner rate above the staff rate only when all three hold:
     1. there is real unmet billable demand
     2. the time comes back in usable blocks, not scattered minutes
     3. the rate is net margin per billable hour, not the billing rate

     Otherwise use the staff rate, and put "evenings back" in `noted_benefits`. Avoiding a hire is valued at the staff rate, not added separately.
2. **Revenue won or kept:** `recovered_revenue_annual × gross_margin × recapture_rate`.
3. **Money not lost:** `losses_avoided_annual × share_avoided`. This is actual out-of-pocket loss: write-offs, chargebacks, scrapped parts, non-recoverable refunds. Not list price, and nothing already counted as revenue.
4. **Noted, not priced:** `noted_benefits`, a list of the owner's words. Shown, never added.

**`unpriced_drivers`:** an optional dict of drivers that apply but couldn't be estimated, for example `{"revenue": "owner couldn't estimate"}`. Printed as "not priced (…)" so the total is never read as complete.

**Double-counting traps** (one home: `references/interview.md`):
1. Owner time at a billable rate while also counting recovered revenue that needs those same hours.
2. Rework counted in minutes and again in losses.
3. The same botched order counted in both revenue and losses.

Saving labour and winning margin on the same queue are distinct and both count.

**Evidence:**
- **Labour weight:**
  - 1.0 when volume came from an export and owner and staff hours don't conflict
  - otherwise by hours claims: agree 0.85, single 0.65; conflicting is valued at 0.65 and is Not ready
- **Revenue and losses weight:** 0.65, unless backed by records (`money_evidence: "records"`), which makes it 1.0.
- The hours check always runs, even with an export. The blocker reads: "hours: owner says X, staff say Y; reconcile what each is counting and confirm with a quick time check". `hours_claims` is updated only when both sides agree on the same scope.

**Output:**
- The quote blocker says "total first-year cost (build plus a year of running)".
- `--markdown` keeps the summary table and prints a short value breakdown per scored job underneath: priced drivers with their basis, not-priced drivers, and noted benefits.
- The quadrant chart only appears when 2 or more jobs have quotes. The x-axis reads "Higher build cost vs value --> Lower build cost vs value".

The four outcomes are unchanged.

## 4. Report (`references/report.md`)

- The answer addresses the problem the owner raised, or says why not and what would.
- The value section shows the script's breakdown and the noted benefits in the owner's words.
- Staff concerns go in anonymously under "Case against" or "What would change the answer", with how to bring staff into the pilot.
- The never and tone questions move to the interview's set-up step (one home).
- The next step matches the owner's actual problem. Never pitch a quote for a job they didn't want. For "not yet", offer a short call only if they want help with the root bottleneck.
- The assistant brief and the "where it goes" line are unchanged from v0.3.
